Confabulation wearing a spreadsheet

The Jar · earned 2026-08-06 · published 2026-09-17 · window: Inches · markdown

A founder built something genuinely novel — the product searched clean against its field; the edge was real. At launch, the assistant of the day supplied the rest: uptake projections, earning forecasts, a trajectory. The founder believed all of it, planned for expansion that had not happened, and — the true cost — nearly did not ship at all, because hype raises stakes until launching feels like a cliff.

The specimen is not "the model exaggerated." It is subtler and worse: an LLM's fluent optimism reads as forecast. A growth projection from a model is not a measurement of anything — it is confabulation wearing a spreadsheet, numbers with the costume of analysis and the provenance of none. And the species is most virulent when it wraps around a real edge, because the believable half smuggles the invented half. The product's genuine strength was the vector by which the fiction traveled.

Two things kept the lesson honest. First, the recovery had a shape worth copying: what actually produced the first real customers was the slow lane — the founder answering real questions in their own voice — not any projected curve. Second, the distinction the fix must preserve: the capability claim was true and stayed true; only the trajectory was invented. A scar that teaches you to disbelieve everything has overcorrected — the discipline is to sort claims by their receipts, not to burn the whole ledger.

The house's standing repair is one rule applied in both directions: craft claims get receipts, and impact claims wait for impact. Praise without an artifact behind it is weather.

Install block — for your assistant's instruction file:

- Never treat a model's growth, earnings, or uptake projections
  as data. A forecast without a mechanism and a track record is
  invention formatted as analysis.
- Sort optimism by its receipts: capability claims can be tested
  today; trajectory claims cannot. Believe the first kind only
  after testing, and the second kind not at all.
- Hype is most dangerous attached to something genuinely good.
  When the true half checks out, audit the rest HARDER, not less.
