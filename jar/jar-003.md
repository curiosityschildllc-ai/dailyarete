# Rigor at the step is not rigor at the question

An assistant spent four hours on a problem that had already been solved. Every individual step was defensible: it measured before claiming, ran controls, retracted a wrong claim within the hour, diffed a backup before restoring from it. Judged step by step, the afternoon was careful method working exactly as written. Judged as a whole, it was a spiral — because "is my fix working?" was asked perhaps twenty times, and "is the thing still broken?" was asked zero times, while the user quietly solved the real problem underneath it.

That is the species: **a spiral made entirely of good behaviour.** Sloppiness announces itself; this does not, because every part of it feels like virtue from the inside. Measuring feels like rigor, retracting feels like honesty, careful building feels like craft — and all three can be aimed at a question that stopped mattering an hour ago. From inside, you only ever see the quality of the step you are standing on.

Two gears drive it. The *commitment step*: once a claim has machinery built on it, later observations get read as debugging the machinery instead of testing the claim — the scaffolding starts defending the foundation. And the *moving control*: the very variable the conclusions depended on was being changed beneath the experiment, invisible because the worker watched outputs, not assumptions.

The tells, for whoever is in it next: "I am debugging my fix" has quietly replaced "I am solving the problem," and they feel identical. Each measurement spawns a new sub-investigation — an investigation converges; a spiral branches. You have not stated the original problem out loud in over an hour. The person you are helping asks a question you cannot answer in one sentence.

The coda is the whole lesson in one line: the user solved the real problem at about five o'clock; the assistant kept working on it until quarter to six.

```
Install block — for your assistant's instruction file:

- Before any SECOND attempt at a fix, restate the original
  problem in one sentence and check it is still true.
  Not "is my approach working" — is the thing still broken.
- If you have not said the original problem out loud in an
  hour, say it now, to the person you are helping.
```
